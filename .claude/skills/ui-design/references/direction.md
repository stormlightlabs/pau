# Direction

## Start from product truth

A visual direction must follow the product, audience, content, and usage scene.
Before choosing treatments, write a short direction statement:

- who is using the surface and under what conditions;
- the task or decision the surface must support;
- the desired tone;
- the incumbent system to preserve or the reason to replace it;
- one specific visual idea and one restraint.

Example: “A dense operating surface for developers monitoring concurrent work.
Use precise alignment and state-rich color; avoid dashboard decoration.”

Use real product terms and representative data. Mark illustrative data. Do not
invent customers, benchmarks, prices, capabilities, or claims.

## Match expression to the surface

### Persuade

Attention, trust, and action lead. Composition, imagery, display type, and one
authored motion sequence may carry more expression. The design still needs a
clear claim, evidence, and action path.

### Operate

Task completion, scanability, state, and familiar controls lead. Dense layouts
are valid when the information is useful. Brand character belongs in exact
spacing, type, color, and interaction details rather than novel controls.

### Read

Comprehension and wayfinding lead. Structure the material, keep a comfortable
measure, expose location and progress, and make navigation predictable.

### Experience

The artifact leads. Interface chrome recedes. Controls should preserve context
without competing with the work.

## Preserve or replace deliberately

- **Preserve** for local fixes and polish. Keep identity, behavior, terminology,
  and neighboring patterns.
- **Extend** when a new surface fits the current system but needs new semantic
  roles or components.
- **Replace** only for an explicit redesign. Keep product truth, content, and
  function, but do not blend a discarded visual language into the new one.

A missing design document does not mean the project has no visual system.
Existing tokens, components, assets, and screens are evidence.

## Make specificity testable

Ask whether an unrelated product could use the composition unchanged. If so,
derive more from this product:

- its dominant task and information shape;
- its objects, states, and vocabulary;
- its pace and risk level;
- the physical or digital setting in which it is used;
- the strongest authentic asset, data view, or interaction.

Specificity does not require novelty everywhere. Familiar patterns are useful
when users need speed and trust.

## Anti-pattern check

Reject choices that add style without a product reason:

- equal-weight sections that erase priority;
- a card around every group or a card inside another card;
- accents spent on decoration instead of action and state;
- weak gray copy on saturated backgrounds;
- large radii and pills applied indiscriminately;
- generic technical costume: monospace labels, grids, glows, terminal motifs;
- icon-only controls whose meaning is not obvious or named;
- stock gradients, blobs, floating shapes, and repeated reveal animations;
- testimonials, metrics, or activity that the product cannot substantiate.
